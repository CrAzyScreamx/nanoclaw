# The grammar tells

Each of these is something a fluent speaker never gets wrong and a machine often does.

## Counting: numbers invert for 3–10

The form ending in `-ה` goes with **masculine** nouns.

| | with a masculine noun | with a feminine noun |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `פריט אחד` | `שורה אחת` |
| 2 | `שני פריטים` | `שתי שורות` |
| 3 | `שלושה פריטים` | `שלוש שורות` |
| 4 | `ארבעה פריטים` | `ארבע שורות` |
| 5 | `חמישה פריטים` | `חמש שורות` |
| 6 | `שישה פריטים` | `שש שורות` |
| 7 | `שבעה פריטים` | `שבע שורות` |
| 8 | `שמונה פריטים` | `שמונה שורות` |
| 9 | `תשעה פריטים` | `תשע שורות` |
| 10 | `עשרה פריטים` | `עשר שורות` |

`1` follows its noun (`פריט אחד`); `2`–`10` precede it. Above 10 the inversion stops mattering
in speech and most writing — `12 פריטים` is what everyone says.

A digit in parentheses sidesteps the whole thing: `פריטים (3)`. That is why structured output
uses digits — the trap only bites prose written freehand.

## The rest

- **`כדי`, never `בכדי`.** `בכדי` traditionally means "in vain", and to an Israeli reader it
  marks the writer as trying too hard.
- **The definite article contracts** after `ב`/`ל`/`כ`, and `מן ה־` becomes `מה־`:
  `בבית` not `בהבית`, `לחדר` not `להחדר`, `מהמטבח` not `מןהמטבח`.
- **Final letters.** `ם ן ץ ף ך` end a word; `מ נ צ פ כ` do not. Check any word you assembled
  from parts rather than recalled whole.
- **No nikud.** Unvocalized text is the norm in every register of modern written Hebrew. Add
  diacritics only when asked, or for a real ambiguity a reader would trip on.
- **Hebrew letter numerals are for days and dates only** — `יום א'`, `ה' בטבת`. Counts,
  quantities, prices, list numbering, and IDs use Western digits: `3`, not `ג'`.
- **Don't "correct" someone's spelling back at them.** If a person wrote `קוטג'` or a nonstandard
  variant, that is the word they used — keep it when you're quoting them or passing it to a tool.
  Standard spelling applies to prose you author, not to their words.
- **Plurals of loanwords take Hebrew endings**: `אפליקציות`, `לינקים`, `קבצים` — not an English
  `-s`.
